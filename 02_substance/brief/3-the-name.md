# Part 3: the name

From the [Space Peak concept brief](../Space-Peak-Concept-Brief.md), a speculative venture thesis, not an engineering proposal.

## Why "Space Peak" is the right name

The formula behind Silicon Valley: *the field* + *the type of place*, both words boring, poetry emerging on its own. The minute a name tries to be clever it becomes a condo development (RIP "Cloudstation Peak").

| Word | Why it fits |
| --- | --- |
| **Space** | The field, not one product inside it. Not "Aero", nobody there builds airplanes. Not "Radar", too narrow. And not "Satellite", which an earlier draft of this brief argued for. A satellite is a product, and the corridor does launch, ground systems and space science as well as satellites. The field name covers all of that. The product name covers a quarter of it |
| **Peak**, culturally | Geographically the city sits in a valley at 6,035 ft (unverified). But *culturally* Peak is the identity Colorado Springs actually chose. It is the "Pikes Peak Region," "Pikes Peak or Bust," "America the Beautiful," whose opening lines came to Katharine Lee Bates on the summit. "Colorado Springs" was itself borrowed branding in 1871 (unverified; the springs are in Manitou). The city has always named itself after the thing it looks *up* at |
| **Peak**, in the mass-driver future | "Peak" stops being a metaphor and becomes the **job description**, peaks as launch sites, the way "valley" meant fabs |

**Naming lineage of this idea:** Silicon Valley → Space Peak. Each a bet on the era's defining field, named after the type of place that field gets built in.

It also matches its siblings. Photon Mountain and Fusion Canyon name a field plus a landform. "Satellite Peak" would have been the one product name in a family of fields.
